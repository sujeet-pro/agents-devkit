# Status Report Guidelines

Guidelines for writing and reviewing sprint, weekly, or project status reports. A status report communicates progress, blockers, and plans to stakeholders who need to make decisions based on the team's current state.

**Audience**: Engineering managers, product managers, and leadership who need a quick, honest picture of progress and risks.

---

## 1. Required Sections

Every status report must include the following sections in order.

| # | Section | Purpose |
|---|---------|---------|
| 1 | Executive Summary | 2-4 sentences for leadership with overall status indicator |
| 2 | Completed Work | What was shipped this period |
| 3 | In Progress | Current work with status and ETA |
| 4 | Blockers | Issues preventing progress with owners |
| 5 | Key Metrics | Quantitative performance data |
| 6 | Next Period Plan | Upcoming priorities |

Optional sections: Carry-Over, Risks, Highlights & Wins, Notes.

---

## 2. Content Standards

### Executive Summary
- Start with a traffic-light status: On Track / At Risk / Behind.
- 2-4 sentences maximum: what was shipped, completion percentage, headline risk or win.
- Written for non-technical readers.

### Completed Work
- List shipped items by name, not just ticket numbers.
- Include owner and story points or effort estimate.
- Link to tickets for traceability.

### In Progress
- Show percentage complete or meaningful progress indicator.
- Include ETA and confidence level (on track vs at risk).

### Blockers
- Every blocker must have an owner and a specific action needed.
- Distinguish between technical blockers and dependency blockers.
- If no blockers, state that explicitly — do not omit the section.

### Key Metrics
- Include sprint velocity, bug count, deployment frequency, or other agreed-upon metrics.
- Show trend (up/down/stable) alongside the absolute number.
- Use charts for velocity trends over time.

### Next Period Plan
- Prioritize by P0/P1/P2.
- State planned capacity (story points or number of items).
- Highlight any dependency on other teams.

---

## 3. Common Issues

- **Overly optimistic**: Everything is "on track" until it's suddenly behind. Be honest about risks early.
- **No blockers section**: Omitting blockers suggests either no transparency or no awareness of risks.
- **Activity instead of outcomes**: "Worked on feature X" is activity. "Shipped feature X to 10% of users" is an outcome.
- **Stale metrics**: Reporting the same metrics every week without updating baselines.
- **Missing trends**: A single data point is noise. Show trends over 3-5 periods.

---

## 4. Review Checklist

- [ ] Overall status indicator is present (On Track / At Risk / Behind)
- [ ] Executive summary is 2-4 sentences and readable by non-engineers
- [ ] Completed work lists specific items with owners and tickets
- [ ] In-progress items have ETAs and status indicators
- [ ] Blockers have owners and specific actions needed (or section states "none")
- [ ] Key metrics include trends, not just absolute numbers
- [ ] Next period plan has prioritized items with capacity
- [ ] Report is consistent with previous period (carry-over items accounted for)
- [ ] Charts or visual indicators are used for metrics and velocity
