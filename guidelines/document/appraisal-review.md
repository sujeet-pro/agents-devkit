# Appraisal / Performance Review Document Guidelines

Guidelines for writing and reviewing performance appraisal documents. These ensure reviews are evidence-based, balanced, and actionable.

---

## 1. Purpose & Audience

Performance reviews serve two audiences: the employee (who needs clear, honest feedback) and the organization (which needs a defensible record of performance assessment). The document must be specific enough that someone unfamiliar with the employee's work can understand their contributions and growth trajectory.

---

## 2. Required Sections

Every performance review document must contain these sections in order:

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **Review Period** | Date range being evaluated (e.g., Q1-Q2 2025, Annual 2025) |
| **Key Accomplishments** | Major contributions with quantified impact metrics |
| **Areas of Strength** | Competencies and behaviors the employee excels at |
| **Growth Areas** | Specific areas where improvement is needed |
| **Goals** | Forward-looking objectives in SMART format |
| **Feedback Incorporation** | How prior review feedback was addressed |
| **Development Plan** | Concrete actions with timeline for growth |

---

## 3. Content Standards

### Key Accomplishments

- Every accomplishment must quantify impact. Vague statements are not acceptable.
  - **Weak**: "Improved system performance"
  - **Strong**: "Reduced P95 API latency from 1200ms to 340ms by redesigning the caching layer, eliminating ~15 customer-reported timeout incidents per week"
- Include the scope: team-level, org-level, or company-level impact.
- Attribute credit accurately. Distinguish between leading, contributing to, and supporting an initiative.
- Limit to 3-6 accomplishments. More dilutes significance; fewer suggests insufficient scope.

### Areas of Strength

- Ground each strength in observable behavior, not personality traits.
  - **Weak**: "Is a team player"
  - **Strong**: "Consistently unblocks teammates by offering targeted code reviews within 4 hours of request, often identifying subtle issues others miss"
- Strengths should outnumber or equal growth areas to keep the review balanced.

### Growth Areas

- Frame growth areas as opportunities with specific examples, not character judgments.
  - **Weak**: "Needs to communicate better"
  - **Strong**: "Design documents would benefit from explicit trade-off analysis. The auth service proposal omitted latency implications of the chosen approach, which surfaced late in implementation"
- Each growth area must pair with a concrete suggestion for improvement.
- Limit to 2-4 growth areas. More than 4 overwhelms and signals a larger performance issue that requires a different conversation.

### Goals (SMART Format)

Every goal must satisfy all five SMART criteria:

| Criterion | Requirement | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Specific** | Precisely what will be accomplished | "Lead the migration of the payments service to the new event-driven architecture" |
| **Measurable** | How success is quantified | "Processing latency under 200ms at P99, zero data loss during migration" |
| **Achievable** | Realistic given resources and constraints | Appropriate for the employee's level and available bandwidth |
| **Relevant** | Aligned to team/org objectives | Tied to a stated team priority or OKR |
| **Time-bound** | Clear deadline or milestone dates | "Complete by end of Q3 2025 with checkpoint review at mid-Q2" |

- Include 3-5 goals. Fewer than 3 lacks ambition; more than 5 lacks focus.
- At least one goal should target a stated growth area.

### Feedback Incorporation

- Reference specific feedback from the prior review cycle.
- For each item, state what was done and the result.
  - "Prior feedback: Improve estimation accuracy. Action taken: Adopted story-point calibration sessions. Result: Sprint commitment accuracy improved from 62% to 88% over 3 sprints"
- If feedback was not addressed, acknowledge it and explain why.

### Development Plan

- Each development action must have: an activity, a timeline, and a success indicator.
  - "Complete the distributed systems design course by end of Q2. Apply learnings by leading the design review for the queue service migration in Q3"
- Include a mix of learning activities (courses, reading, mentoring) and stretch assignments (leading a project, presenting to stakeholders).
- Development plans should be reviewed at the mid-cycle check-in, not only at the next annual review.

---

## 4. Structure & Flow

- Open with the review period and a 1-2 sentence summary of overall performance level.
- Present accomplishments before growth areas (lead with what went well).
- Goals should flow naturally from the combination of strengths (leverage) and growth areas (address).
- The development plan should close the document, establishing forward momentum.
- Use consistent formatting: bullet points for lists, tables for structured data.

---

## 5. Common Issues

- **Recency bias**: Citing only accomplishments from the last month. Cover the full review period.
- **Halo/horn effect**: Letting one strong (or weak) project color the entire review.
- **Vague praise**: "Great job this year" conveys nothing. Always be specific.
- **Sandwich feedback**: Burying criticism between compliments reduces clarity. Be direct and respectful.
- **Copy-paste goals**: Goals identical to last cycle suggest no progression. Goals should evolve.
- **Missing metrics**: If an accomplishment cannot be quantified, it likely needs reframing or supporting evidence.
- **Comparison to peers**: Review the individual against expectations for their role/level, not against teammates.

---

## 6. Review Checklist

- [ ] Review period is explicitly stated
- [ ] Each accomplishment includes quantified impact
- [ ] Accomplishments cover the full review period, not just recent work
- [ ] Strengths are grounded in observable behavior
- [ ] Growth areas include specific examples and improvement suggestions
- [ ] Growth areas are balanced with strengths (not lopsided)
- [ ] All goals satisfy every SMART criterion
- [ ] At least one goal addresses a stated growth area
- [ ] Prior feedback is referenced with actions taken and results
- [ ] Development plan includes activities, timelines, and success indicators
- [ ] Tone is direct, respectful, and professional throughout
- [ ] No vague or unsubstantiated claims remain
